## Comparison with the other implementations

Measured in one session on a single machine, each implementation holding its own
cryptographic backend constant. Overhead ratios are within-language and are the
comparable quantity; absolute latencies are not, since the four harnesses differ
in the non-cryptographic work they perform per handshake.

| Metric                 | py-libp2p | js-libp2p-noise     | rust-libp2p       | nim-libp2p |
| ---------------------- | --------- | ------------------- | ----------------- | ---------- |
| KEM library            | kyber-py  | @noble/post-quantum | RustCrypto ml-kem | BoringSSL  |
| vs classical overhead  | 10.5x     | 1.47x               | 1.30x             | 1.13x      |
| KEM share of handshake | ~87%      | ~32%                | not measured      | ~8%        |

py-libp2p is the outlier because kyber-py performs the lattice arithmetic in pure
Python, which is also why it has the most to gain from the liboqs backend: the KEM
is roughly 87% of the handshake, so substituting a C implementation addresses
almost the whole cost.

An earlier version of this table reported js-libp2p at ~5x. That figure compared a
native-backend classical handshake against a pure-JavaScript hybrid one, so it
attributed a change of crypto backend to the KEM.
